
# Daniel Atilano Barbosa, Ph.D.

## Social and Cognitive Neuroscientist

### *Understanding how social experiences shape the human brain through multimodal neuroimaging*

I am a **Social and Cognitive Neuroscientist** and psychologist whose research seeks to understand how social experiences become biologically embedded in the human brain. My work integrates **social neuroscience**, **cognitive neuroscience**, **multimodal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)**, and **computational neuroimaging** to investigate the neural mechanisms underlying empathy, social identity, moral cognition, trauma, and mental health.

My research is motivated by socially relevant questions at the intersection of neuroscience, psychology, and public health. I am particularly interested in understanding how violence, discrimination, chronic adversity, and other social determinants influence brain organization, cognition, emotional functioning, and psychological well-being across diverse and historically underrepresented populations.

Rather than studying the brain in isolation, I investigate how biological and social processes interact to shape human behavior. My goal is to generate scientifically rigorous and socially meaningful evidence capable of informing mental health interventions, reducing health disparities, and supporting evidence-based public policies.

---

# Research Program

My research program is organized around four complementary research lines.

## Social Neuroscience

Understanding the neural mechanisms underlying empathy, social identity, moral cognition, emotional regulation, and interpersonal behavior using multimodal neuroimaging.

---

## Neuroimaging of Social Vulnerability

Investigating how violence, trauma, discrimination, substance use, and adverse social environments influence structural and functional brain organization.

---

## Population Neuroimaging

Developing neuroimaging methodologies that improve the representation of ethnically, culturally, and socially diverse populations while reducing WEIRD bias in neuroscience research.

---

## Translational Neuroscience for Mental Health

Applying multimodal neuroimaging to identify neurobiological mechanisms that contribute to prevention, diagnosis, and intervention strategies for vulnerable populations.

---

# Research Vision

My long-term vision is to establish an internationally recognized research program in **Social and Cognitive Neuroscience** dedicated to understanding how social environments shape brain organization across the lifespan.

Through interdisciplinary collaborations integrating psychology, neuroscience, neuroimaging, and computational methods, I aim to generate knowledge that advances neuroscience while improving mental health, promoting social equity, and informing evidence-based public policy for vulnerable populations.

# Scientific Philosophy

> *Understanding the human brain requires understanding the social environments in which people live and the emotional experiences that shape human life.*

I believe neuroscience should not only explain how the brain works, but also contribute to addressing the societal challenges that shape mental health and human behavior. My research therefore combines rigorous neuroimaging methodologies with psychologically and socially meaningful questions to advance a more inclusive, equitable, and translational neuroscience.
